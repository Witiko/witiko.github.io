Last month, I visited Canada for the second time, following my trip to
Vancouver during my postgraduate studies for [ACL 2017][8]. This time, I first
spent five days with my family in Banff National Park and then attended the
three-day [_TUG 2026_][4] conference in Calgary.

At TUG, I presented [two][1] [talks][2]. The first, titled [_Expltools: Two
years of LaTeX3 static analysis_][1], covered the latest developments in the
[_expltools_][3] bundle that I have been [blogging about][7] for the past two
years.

In this post, I recount the whole week as I remember it. For more information
about TUG, see also [the conference program][5] and watch the other talks in
[this YouTube playlist][6].

# July 10 and 11: From Brno to Banff
On Friday, July 10, I travelled by train from Brno, Czechia, to Prague,
where I spent the evening with my brother and mom at _Klášterní šenk_ before
spending the night at the Charles University dormitories.

Early the next morning, the three of us flew from Prague to Calgary, Canada,
via Frankfurt. Despite roughly 14 hours of travel, we arrived in Calgary the

# July 18: The second day of the conference, Horseshoe Canyon, Royal Tyrrell Museum, and Willow Creek Hoodoos
The second day of the conference was much richer thematically, moving beyond
accessibility to other developments in the TeX world.

In the morning, I gave my first talk, titled [_Expltools: Two years of LaTeX3
static analysis_][1] (see also [the presentation slides][10]), in which I
discussed the latest developments in my [_expltools_][3] bundle.

  {% include image.html url="tug2026-tubman-016.jpg"
     description="Me, explaining new algorithms added to expltools in the past year"
     attribution="Jim Tubman" %}

  {% include youtube.html id="M9h7BbJMatg"
     description="“Expltools: Two years of LaTeX3 static analysis” on YouTube" %}

During the coffee break after my talk, I spoke with Matthew Skala, who offers
[an online machine learning course][9] and was interested in further exploring
an experiment that I had presented in Section 4 of my talk.

I also spoke with Mitchell Gerrard, who was interested in the topics from the
talk through our shared interest in static analysis of computer programs and
later that day gave [their own talk on literate programming][11].

  {% include image.html url="IMG_20260718_131528.jpg"
     description="Me speaking with Mitchell Gerrard about static analysis of computer programs"
     attribution="Pavneet Arora" %}

In the afternoon, I gave my second talk, titled [_No more backtracking:
Abbreviations in the Markdown package for TeX_][2] (see also [the presentation
slides][12], [the preprint][13], and the [finished article][14] in _TUGboat_
47:2), in which I discussed a new feature in my [_markdown_][15] package for
automatically formatting acronyms in Markdown documents.

  {% include youtube.html id="gcKV-9Z0t4g"
     description="“No more backtracking: Abbreviations in the Markdown package for TeX” on YouTube" %}

After the talk, I spoke with Erik Nijenhuis and Boris Veytsman, both of whom
suggested extracting the underlying algorithm and generalizing it for use in
LaTeX documents more broadly. I outlined what this would entail in Section 7,
titled _Future work_, of the [_TUGboat_ article][14].

At the end of the day, the _TUG Annual General Meeting_ took place, chaired by
Karl Berry. I also met Karl in person for the first time, despite having
corresponded with him for well over a decade.

My brother and mom travelled to _Drumheller_, where they visited _Horseshoe
Canyon_, the _Royal Tyrrell Museum_ in _Midland Provincial Park_, and the
_Willow Creek Hoodoos_.

The flight back to Prague took another 14 hours, but this time the time
difference worked against us. We arrived the following morning, and I then took
a train to Brno, where I arrived late that same night to my wife and son.

# Parting thoughts
This summer was crazy. Starting at the end of June, I [attended _OSSConf_
in Slovakia][16] for the first time; thanks to Erik Nijenhuis for inviting the TUG
participants to OSSConf 2027 on behalf of Pavel Stříž and me! I then spent a
week with my family and in-laws in Central Bohemia before heading to TUG.

Almost immediately after returning home from TUG, I [headed to my first
international LARP][17], followed by cooking at a summer camp and renovating
our own kitchen. Altogether, that made for almost two months of near-continuous
social activity, which I found particularly exhausting.

  {% include image.html url="20260825_175100.jpg"
     description="Our kitchen in the middle of renovations" %}

For those reasons, I arrived at TUG 2026 more stressed than usual, and much of
the conference passed in more of a blur than I would have liked. Despite that,
and despite the many competing experiences, TUG 2026 was still the highlight
of my summer. I thoroughly enjoyed it and am looking forward both to future TUG
conferences and, perhaps, to seeing more of Canada someday.
